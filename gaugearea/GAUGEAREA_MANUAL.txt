1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CORE PURPOSE
================================================================================
GaugeArea is an industrial-grade engineering calculator and workshop decision-support 
system designed for electric motor rewinding and transformer repair shops.

When an electric motor or transformer stator requires rewinding, technicians often 
face situations where the original specified wire gauge (for example, a heavy 17 SWG 
or 14 SWG magnet wire) is either:
  a) Out of stock in the workshop inventory,
  b) Too rigid or thick to feed manually into narrow, deep stator slots without 
     scratching the delicate enamel insulation, or
  c) Originally wound with multiple parallel strands that need to be re-engineered 
     using locally available spool sizes.

GaugeArea solves this challenge by instantaneously computing mathematically exact 
and practical multi-wire parallel combinations (e.g., winding 2, 3, 4, 5, or more 
thinner wires in hand) that match the exact total copper cross-sectional area of 
the original coil.

Crucially, GaugeArea goes far beyond simplistic area math by providing true 
workshop engineering analysis:
  - Enamel Volume Slot Fill Shift (alerting technicians if multiple wires will 
    overcrowd the stator slot).
  - Phase Resistance Ratio (guaranteeing that motor torque, operating temperature, 
    and rated horsepower remain within design limits).
  - Workshop Practicality Score (ranking combinations by whether all wires are the 
    same gauge, using common workshop stock, and minimizing winding complexity).

2. WHY PARALLEL WIRE REWINDING IS NECESSARY (THE WORKSHOP PROBLEM)
================================================================================
In alternating current (AC) induction motors, direct current (DC) armatures, and 
transformers, coil windings carry electrical current to establish the stator magnetic 
flux. 

Key considerations:
1. COPPER CROSS-SECTIONAL AREA (A):
   Determines current-carrying capacity ($I = J \times A$, where $J$ is current 
   density, typically 3.5 to 6.5 A/mm² for air-cooled enclosed motors).
   - If total copper area is REDUCED: Electrical resistance increases ($R \propto 1/A$). 
     $I^2 R$ copper losses increase dramatically as heat, causing thermal breakdown 
     of the insulation varnish and eventual motor burnout.
   - If total copper area is EXCESSIVELY INCREASED: While electrical resistance drops, 
     the physical volume occupied by the copper and its enamel insulation exceeds 
     the slot area. The coils will physically not fit into the stator slots, or 
     wedges cannot be driven in without crimping and short-circuiting the wire.

2. WIRES "IN HAND" (PARALLEL STRANDS):
   Winding multiple thinner wires simultaneously (known in the trade as "two in hand", 
   "three in hand", "four in hand") provides significant mechanical advantages:
   - High Flexibility: Thinner wires bend easily around tight coil end-turns.
   - Ease of Insertion: Strands slip through narrow slot openings one by one.
   - Reduced Skin Effect: In high-frequency or inverter-fed (VFD) motors, parallel 
     smaller conductors reduce eddy current losses.

3. STEP-BY-STEP WORKFLOW & OPERATING GUIDE
================================================================================
STEP 1: INPUT ORIGINAL WIRE SPECIFICATIONS
- Enter the original coil specifications found during motor strip-down.
- Supports single wires or original multi-wire bundles (e.g., 2× 20 SWG).
- Unit toggle: Choose SWG (Standard Wire Gauge), AWG (American Wire Gauge), 
  or bare diameter in millimeters (mm) with 0.001 mm precision.
- Real-time display updates showing total target copper area ($A_{target}$) and 
  equivalent single conductor diameter.

STEP 2: CONSTRAIN STRAND COUNT (OPTIONAL)
- Select "Auto (Any)" for an exhaustive combinatorial search across all practical 
  strand counts.
- Or click "2W", "3W", "4W", "5W", "6W", or type any custom strand count (1 to 99).

STEP 3: SET TOLERANCE WINDOW
- Choose "Symmetric (±%)" with fast presets (0% Exact, ±0.2%, ±0.5%, ±1%, ±2%, 
  ±3%, ±5%, ±8%) or enter any custom decimal percentage (e.g. 1.25%).
- Or choose "Min% – Max%" for asymmetric tolerance (e.g., 98% minimum to 104% maximum).

STEP 4: ANALYZE AND FILTER COMBINATIONS
- Review computed combinations sorted by "Workshop Best (Score & Practicality)".
- Filter by:
  * "Same Wire Size" (e.g., 4 strands of 23 SWG — easiest to wind from a single rack).
  * "Thicker (1–3 Strands)" (minimal number of wires to manage).
  * "Flexible (4+ Strands)" (maximum pliability for tight slots).
  * "Ideal (±2%)" vs "Usable (±5%)".

STEP 5: GENERATE & PRINT WORKSHOP JOB CARD
- Click the "Print / Job Card" button on any selected combination.
- Generates a high-contrast, clean black-and-white printable workshop card with:
  * Job Card reference number, date, and motor identification fields.
  * Original vs. Replacement specifications table.
  * Total copper area, difference in mm², and percentage shift.
  * Slot fill shift warning and phase resistance ratio.
  * Technician verification checklist (enamel grade, surge test, resistance test).

================================================================================

4.1 BARE CONDUCTOR DIAMETER TO CROSS-SECTIONAL AREA
--------------------------------------------------
For any round bare wire with diameter $d$ in millimeters (mm):

               π × d²
    A (mm²) = --------  ≈ 0.7853981633974483 × d²
                 4

Where:
  d = bare conductor diameter in mm
  A = cross-sectional copper area in mm²

Note on Circular Mils (North American AWG practice):
  Area (circular mils) = (d in inches × 1000)² = d_mils²
  1 mm² ≈ 1973.525 circular mils.

4.2 TOTAL TARGET CROSS-SECTIONAL AREA (A_target)
-----------------------------------------------
When an original coil consists of multiple parallel strands or different wire sizes 
wound together in hand:

                 k
    A_target =  ∑  ( N_i × A_i )
                i=1

Where:
  k   = number of distinct wire sizes in the original specification
  N_i = quantity/strands of wire size i
  A_i = cross-sectional area of wire size i in mm²

Example:
  Original specification = 1× 18 SWG (1.1675 mm²) + 1× 20 SWG (0.6567 mm²)
  A_target = 1.1675 + 0.6567 = 1.8242 mm²

4.3 EQUIVALENT SINGLE CONDUCTOR DIAMETER (d_eq)
-----------------------------------------------
The theoretical single solid conductor having identical copper cross-sectional area:

                 ________________
                /   4 × A_target
    d_eq (mm) =/  ----------------  =  2 × √( A_target / π )
             \/          π

4.4 AREA DIFFERENCE AND PERCENTAGE DEVIATION
--------------------------------------------
For any proposed replacement combination with total computed area $A_{calc}$:

    Absolute Difference (ΔA):
        ΔA (mm²) = A_{calc} - A_{target}

    Percentage Deviation (%Δ):
                   A_{calc} - A_{target}
        %Δ (%) =  ----------------------- × 100%
                         A_{target}

    Ratio to Target:
                     A_{calc}
        Area Ratio = -------- × 100%
                     A_{target}

Workshop Acceptance Standards:
  - Strict / Ideal    : ±0.0% to ±2.0%
  - Standard Usable   : ±2.1% to ±5.0%
  - Borderline        : ±5.1% to ±8.0% (requires slot fill & cooling verification)
  - Reject / Hazard   : > ±8.0% (risk of severe overheating or mechanical refusal)

4.5 DC CONDUCTOR RESISTANCE & PHASE RESISTANCE RATIO
----------------------------------------------------
The DC resistance of an electrical coil at 20°C is governed by Pouillet's Law:

            L
    R = ρ × -
            A

Where:
  R = resistance in Ohms (Ω)
  ρ = electrical resistivity of annealed copper at 20°C 
      (International Annealed Copper Standard, IACS 100%):
      ρ = 1.7241 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m = 0.017241 Ω·mm²/m
  L = total conductor length per phase in meters (m)
  A = total cross-sectional copper area in mm²

Resistance per kilometer of single bare conductor (Ω/km at 20°C):
               17.241
    R_km (Ω) = ------
                 A

Phase Resistance Ratio (R_ratio):
Because length $L$ of the stator turns remains identical when rewinding the motor, 
the ratio of new winding phase resistance to original winding phase resistance is 
inversely proportional to copper area:

               R_{new}       A_{target}
    R_ratio = ----------  =  ----------
              R_{original}    A_{calc}

Implications:
  - If R_ratio < 1.00 : New winding has LESS resistance. Lower I²R heating, motor 
                        runs cooler, slight increase in starting torque.
  - If R_ratio > 1.00 : New winding has MORE resistance. Higher I²R heating, 
                        higher phase voltage drop, slight reduction in efficiency.

4.6 COPPER ENAMEL INSULATION BUILD & STATOR SLOT FILL SHIFT
-----------------------------------------------------------
A critical danger in rewinding: replacing 1 thick wire with multiple thinner wires 
multiplies the total surface area of insulation. Even if copper area is identical, 
the total physical volume of the coil increases due to multiple layers of enamel.

Wire insulation standard: Dual-coat Polyester-imide + Polyamide-imide (Class H / 200°C 
Grade 2 / Heavy Build according to IEC 60317 / NEMA MW 35-C).

Radial Enamel Thickness ($t_{enamel}$):
  Typically 0.020 mm to 0.055 mm depending on wire gauge diameter.
  Overall outer diameter ($d_{outer}$):
      d_{outer} = d_{bare} + 2 × t_{enamel}

Total Outer Area Occupied per Strand:
                     π × (d_{outer})²
      A_{outer}   =  ----------------
                            4

Enamel Volume Slot Fill Shift (%):
Let $A_{outer, orig}$ be the outer insulated area of the original coil, and 
$A_{outer, new}$ be the sum of outer insulated areas of the parallel replacement wires:

                       ∑ ( A_{outer, new, i} ) - A_{outer, orig}
    Slot Fill Shift = ------------------------------------------- × 100%
                                   A_{outer, orig}

Rule of Thumb for Stator Slots:
  - Shift +0% to +15% : Safe for all stators.
  - Shift +15% to +25%: Moderate; requires firm wooden/fiber drift during winding.
  - Shift > +30%      : High risk of slot fill overflow in tight stators; 
                        inspect slot dimension and slot insulation paper thickness.

4.7 BARE COPPER MASS & SPOOL WEIGHT CALCULATIONS
------------------------------------------------
Copper physical density at 20°C:
    δ = 8.89 g/cm³ = 8890 kg/m³

Weight of copper wire per 1000 meters (kg/km):
    Weight per 1000 m (kg) = A (mm²) × 8.89

Weight of replacement coil vs original coil:
    Weight Ratio ≈ A_{calc} / A_{target}

5. WORKSHOP PRACTICALITY SCORING RUBRIC (ALGORITHM EXPLAINED)
================================================================================
The "Workshop Score" is an algorithm (0 to 100 points) calibrated against real-world 
armature rewinding workshop practices:

Base Score: 100 Points

1. AREA ACCURACY PENALTY:
   - Penalty = |%Δ| × 12 points per 1% error.
   - Example: 0.2% error loses 2.4 points; 3.0% error loses 36 points.

2. HOMOGENEITY BONUS / PENALTY (Same Wire Size):
   - +15 Points if all parallel strands use the identical wire gauge (e.g., 4× 22 SWG).
     Why: Technicians can draw all wires from a single reel or identical spools 
     mounted on the wire rack. Equal tension across all strands is guaranteed.
   - -10 Points if combination uses 3 or more distinct wire gauges (heterogeneous).

3. STRAND COUNT ERGONOMICS:
   - 2 Strands: +8 Points (sweet spot for motor rewinding — easy to handle).
   - 3 Strands: +6 Points.
   - 4 Strands: +4 Points.
   - 5 Strands: +1 Point.
   - 6 to 8 Strands: -8 Points (wire bunching and twisting risk).
   - 9+ Strands: -20 Points (extremely difficult to wind by hand without crossing).

4. GAUGE COMMONALITY:
   - Common industrial gauges (18 to 28 SWG): +5 Points.
   - Rare / heavy or ultra-fine gauges (below 12 SWG or above 36 SWG): -10 Points.

5. ENAMEL SLOT FILL PENALTY:
   - If slot fill shift > +25%: -10 Points.
   - If slot fill shift > +35%: -20 Points.

Final score is clamped between 0 and 100. Combinations scoring ≥ 85 are badged 
as "Workshop Best / Top Pick".

6. WIRE GAUGE STANDARDS: SWG vs. AWG vs. METRIC (mm)
================================================================================
1. SWG (British Standard Imperial Standard Wire Gauge, BS 3737):
   - Dominant in the United Kingdom, India, Pakistan, Southeast Asia, Australia, 
     and the Commonwealth.
   - Fixed step increments from 7/0 (0.500 in / 12.7 mm) down to 50 SWG (0.0010 in / 0.025 mm).

2. AWG (American Wire Gauge / Brown & Sharpe):
   - Dominant in North America, United States, Canada, and Latin America.
   - Defined by geometric progression: the ratio of diameter 36 AWG (0.005 in) 
     to 0000 AWG (0.460 in) across 39 steps is exactly 92, giving step ratio 
     ≈ 1.1229322.

3. METRIC SYSTEM (IEC 60317 bare wire diameter in mm):
   - Standardized in Europe and international ISO standards.
   - Specifies exact conductor nominal diameter (e.g. 0.710 mm, 0.900 mm, 1.400 mm).

GaugeArea maintains full built-in conversion lookup tables for:
  - SWG 8 through SWG 44
  - AWG 8 through AWG 44
  - Continuous millimeter input (0.050 mm to 10.000 mm)

10. CONVERTING TO A NATIVE ANDROID APK (OFFLINE WORKSHOP TABLET)
================================================================================
GaugeArea is fully configured to run offline as a Progressive Web App (PWA) or as 
a native Android application (.apk).

METHOD 1: INSTANT ANDROID APP (NO COMPILATION REQUIRED)
1. Open the GaugeArea application URL in Google Chrome on your Android tablet or phone.
2. Tap the three dots menu (⋮) in the top-right corner of Chrome.
3. Tap "Add to Home Screen" or "Install App".
4. GaugeArea will install on your device just like a native app from Google Play, 
   working completely offline with full hardware acceleration.

METHOD 2: NATIVE ANDROID APK VIA CAPACITOR
After downloading the source code ZIP, run the following commands on your computer:
    1. npm install
    2. npm install @capacitor/core @capacitor/cli @capacitor/android
    3. npx cap init "GaugeArea" "com.gaugearea.app"
    4. npm run build
    5. npx cap add android
    6. npx cap open android
This automatically generates an Android Studio project. In Android Studio:
    - Click "Build" > "Build Bundle(s) / APK(s)" > "Build APK(s)".
    - Your compiled `app-debug.apk` or signed release APK will be ready in seconds.
